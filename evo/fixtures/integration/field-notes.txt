Field Notes — Tidal Margin, Day Eleven

The heron returned to the mudflat at dawn. It stood without moving for almost an hour, and then it struck once and swallowed something silver. The light was poor, so I could not see what it had taken.

A second bird, smaller and browner, worked the same channel further out. When the tide turned, they both lifted at once and crossed toward the spit. I do not know whether they had come together or simply shared the water.

Holloway arrived near noon with the new sampling frames. He set them along the high-water line while I logged the temperatures. He said the cold snap had pushed the count down, but the readings did not support him; if anything they were higher than the week before.

By four the wind had risen and the frames began to drag. We weighted them with stones from the bank. It was slow work, and neither of us spoke much, but the job was done before the light failed.
