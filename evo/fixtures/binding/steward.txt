The Steward's Account

Princess Mary came down to the cold storeroom where Dron was waiting for her. The old steward stood with his cap in his hands and would not meet her eyes.

"The peasants will not take the grain," Dron said. "They say they will not leave the village while the old prince's orders stand."

"But they must go," she said. "My father left word that they be moved to Moscow before the French reach the estate."

Dron turned the cap over once. "They do not believe the French are coming, Princess. They believe the masters mean to drive them off the land and keep it."

She looked at him for a long moment, and the lamp threw both their shadows up the wall. "And you, Dron, what do you believe?"

He said nothing for a while. "I believe what the mistress tells me to believe," he answered at last.

Mary felt the whole unfairness of it settle on her shoulders. "Then tell them the grain is theirs," she said. "Tell them I give it freely, and that no one will be driven anywhere."
