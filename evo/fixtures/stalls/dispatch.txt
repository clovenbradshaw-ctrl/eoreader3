Dispatch: The Harbor Vote

The council met on Tuesday to decide the fate of the old harbor works. Alderman Vance opened for the motion to demolish; Councillor Ruiz answered for the trust that wanted them preserved.

Vance argued that the timbers were unsafe and the cost of repair unbearable. Ruiz did not dispute the cost. She said instead that the harbor was the last of its kind on the coast, and that once it was gone it could not be rebuilt.

The gallery was full. When the engineer rose to give her report, the room went quiet. She had surveyed the structure twice, and she would not be drawn beyond what the survey showed: two of the four piers were sound, and two were not.

The vote was held at nine. It failed by a single voice. Afterward Vance and Ruiz left by the same door, still arguing, and the clerk locked the chamber behind them.
